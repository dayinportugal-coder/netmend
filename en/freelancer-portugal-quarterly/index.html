    2026 rules, and the source for each

    A number appears here only when an official page states it. The rule year is 2026. The Portuguese page is the default.

    Relevant income

    70% of services. 20% of goods produced and sold. 20% of hotel, restaurant and drinks services declared as such. VAT is not declared: the FAQ says so. ISS FAQ, 14 January 2026.

    Base and contribution

    The monthly base is 1/3 of the quarter’s relevant income and applies that month and the next two. The self-employed rate is 21.4%. The FAQ’s example: €6,000 of services gives €4,200 relevant income, a €1,400 base and €299.60 a month. Trabalhadores independentes.

    Floor, cap and option

    No income, or a contribution under €20, is set at €20 a month. The monthly base cap is 12 × IAS. The 2026 IAS is €537.13, so the cap is €6,445.56. On the declaration you can fix relevant income from −25% to +25%, in steps of 5. Portaria n.º 480-A/2025.

    Deadlines

    File by the last day of January, April, July and October, for the previous three months. Saturday, Sunday or a public holiday moves it to the next business day. Each month is paid between the 10th and the 20th of the following month. July 2026 notice.

    Also employed

    If monthly relevant income is under 4 × IAS (€2,148.52) and the job pays at least 1 × IAS, an exemption may apply, with the FAQ’s other conditions. Above that, the base is the part over €2,148.52. A remainder contribution under €5 does not count (the FAQ cites Despacho n.º 599/2019).

    VAT exemption, article 53

    Previous-year turnover in Portugal not above €15,000, no exports or related activities, and a seat or home in Portugal. In the current year, exceeding the line by more than 25% (above €18,750) ends the exemption then. Going over €15,000 without passing €18,750 waits until 1 January, with a change declaration within 15 business days. Turnover is without VAT. Decree-Law 35/2025 · Tax FAQ · January 2026 newsletter · Circular 25062.

    IRS withholding, as a reference only

    The page sums the column in the file. It does not apply a rate. Article 101 of the income-tax code tells entities with organised accounts to withhold 23% on activities in the article 151 table and 11.5% on the other category B services described there. The rate applies to income before VAT. Article 101 CIRS.

    What this page does not calculate

    • The exact “Exportar tabela” column names were not confirmed. Recognition is tolerant, and the column map is there when it fails.
    • The 25.2% rate is in question 34 of the January 2026 FAQ. Choose it when you are a sole trader (ENI) or a single-member establishment. The default estimate uses 21.4%.
    • Organised accounting follows taxable profit, not this sum, unless you opted into the quarterly declaration.
    • The calendar moves Saturday, Sunday and mainland national holidays (fixed dates, plus Good Friday and Corpus Christi). It does not include municipal holidays.
    • It does not guess whether an activity is on the article 151 table. Per-client withholding is the column total, not a rate applied here.

    Questions

    Does the receipt file leave my computer?

    No. The CSV or Excel file is read in this browser. The workbook and the calendar are built on your computer. Nothing is sent to Netmend.

    Does this file the Social Security declaration for me?

    No. It is an estimate to prepare the figures. The declaration is filed on Segurança Social Direta. This is not tax advice.

    What do I type on the quarterly declaration?

    The total value of services and the total value of sales, without VAT. Social Security applies 70% or 20% to reach relevant income. This page shows both.

    When is the 2026 quarterly declaration due?

    By the last day of January, April, July and October, for the previous three months. A Saturday, Sunday or public holiday moves the deadline to the next business day. The calendar from this page only moves the weekend.

    What is the 2026 VAT exemption limit for green receipts?

    Article 53 of the VAT code exempts someone whose turnover in Portugal in the previous year was not above €15,000, if the other conditions hold. Going above €18,750 in the current year (more than 25% over €15,000) ends the exemption at that moment.
