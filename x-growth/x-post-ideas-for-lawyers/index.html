X post ideas for lawyers

People look to lawyers on X for a clearer picture of a process: what happens first, what a document is for, what a deadline means. They should not be given personal legal advice in a public thread, and you should not give it.

This page is for solicitors, attorneys and legal practices writing in public. It is information about process, not advice, and it is not a retainer. Do not post anything that could identify a client or a matter. Do not promise outcomes. Follow the publicity and confidentiality rules of your regulator and your firm. If a question needs advice, say so and take it into a proper client channel.

The useful post names a jurisdiction, dates the information, explains one step, and stops before the “apply this to your case” line.

What is usually safe and useful to post

  • A process explainer in one jurisdiction: how a claim usually starts, what a case management conference is, what “without prejudice” is for — in plain language.
  • A document map. What a particular form is, who files it, what it is not.
  • A deadline reminder that is general (for example, a well-known filing season) with a date and a source, plus “check your own position”.
  • How your firm actually works: who the first call is with, whether you do fixed fees for a named service, what you do not handle.
  • A change in a public rule, linked to an official source, summarised without spin.
  • A correction of a common mix-up (criminal vs civil, employee vs contractor) with no punchline at a stranger’s expense.

Example posts you can adapt

Concrete-fact hook · example
In England and Wales, “without prejudice” on an email is not a magic shield for everything you say.

It is a label that can protect genuine settlement discussions from being shown to the court as admissions. It does not hide a threat, and it does not replace advice on your own matter. If you are unsure, ask your solicitor before you hit send.
Question + list · example
If you are about to sign a consumer contract, these are the three clauses I suggest people actually read, slowly:

1. How either side can end it, and how much notice.
2. What you are liable for if you cancel early.
3. Which country’s courts and law it names.

This is general information, not a review of your contract. If the numbers are large, get advice.
Short twist · example
A template contract is not “free” if nobody has checked it against the job in front of you.

The risk sits in the definitions, the liability cap, and the ending clauses. Those are the pages I am asked to look at most often.
Process · example
For a straightforward will, the way we usually work is: a first meeting, a draft you read in your own time, then a signing we supervise.

We will tell you the fee before we start. We do not take instructions for a will over a public reply on X. Write to the practice, or telephone the number on the site.
Jurisdiction note · example
Employment status (employee, worker, self-employed) is not something you can settle with a job title on a business card.

In this jurisdiction it depends on the real working relationship. The official guidance is the starting point; your own facts may need advice. I will not diagnose a stranger’s status in the comments.
What we do not do · example
This practice does not handle criminal defence or family law.

If that is what you need, we will say so quickly and, where we can, point you to a directory of solicitors who do. We would rather send you away than take a matter we do not run.
Very short hook · example
A deadline on a court letter is not a suggestion.

If you have one, speak to a solicitor in your jurisdiction the same day. Do not wait for a social media answer.
Rule change · example
A public consultation on a proposed change to the small-claims limit closed last week. The government’s official page is the source, not a thread. (Example of the shape; cite the real paper if you post this live.)

I will post a short, dated note if and when the rule actually changes. Until then, the current rule is the one that applies.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

What to skip

No war stories that identify a matter, even if you think the details are obscure. No “we always win these”. No legal advice in a reply. No dunking on a litigant in person. Have a partner or compliance lead look at a draft if you are unsure. The free writer can help with tone; it cannot check the law.

FAQ

Can I answer a specific question in the comments?

You can explain a general process and then stop. Personal advice belongs in a client engagement, after a conflict check. Say that clearly.

May I name a judgment?

Public judgments are public. Still be careful with parties’ personal data, and do not imply you acted when you did not. Link the official report if you cite one.

Is this marketing regulated?

In many places, yes. Follow your regulator’s publicity rules. This page is not a substitute for them.
