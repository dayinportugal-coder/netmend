X post ideas for coaches

Motivational one-liners are cheap to write and easy to ignore. Coaches who get useful conversations on X usually post a question they actually ask, a short exercise, or a named framework with steps, and they keep client stories anonymous or clearly fictional.

This is for coaches (life, career, business, executive) who need a public writing habit that does not invent testimonials. It is not medical, therapy or financial advice, and it is not a promise that posting will fill a practice. If a story is based on real work, strip every detail that could identify the person, or use a clearly labelled example.

The posts that tend to get replies are the ones a reader can use in the next ten minutes: a prompt, a distinction, a mistake you see often, or a short sequence. “Believe in yourself” does not give them anything to do.

Specific ideas that fit coaching work

  • The question you ask in session. Write the question, why you ask it, and what a shallow answer sounds like. No client transcript.
  • A two-minute exercise. Timed, concrete, with a stop rule. “Write the decision you are avoiding in one sentence, then write the cost of waiting 90 days.”
  • A named distinction. Busy vs important, advice vs accountability, a goal vs a weekly behaviour. Give the names and one example of each.
  • A framework with three to five steps you actually use, not a reused internet model you cannot explain.
  • A boundary or packaging post. What you do not coach, how you work, how long a typical engagement is, without pressure copy.
  • A correction of a slogan in your field, with a more precise version you stand behind.

Example posts you can adapt

Question + list · example
When someone says “I need more confidence”, I ask three things before we talk about confidence at all:

1. What decision is sitting in your drafts?
2. Who would be affected if you made it this month?
3. What have you already tried, in order?

Confidence is often a label on a delayed decision.
Concrete exercise · example
A ten-minute exercise I use when a plan is vague:

Write the outcome in one sentence.
Write the first visible action that would take under 30 minutes.
Write what you will stop doing this week to make room for it.

If you cannot name the stop, the plan is still a wish.
Short twist · example
Accountability is not a pep talk.

It is a date, a definition of done, and a person who will ask whether it happened. If any of the three is missing, it is just a conversation.
Lessons from work · example
Five career-change conversations in a row stalled in the same place: the person could describe the job they wanted, but not a two-hour test they could run this month.

I now start there. A test you can finish beats a five-year plan you cannot touch.
Distinction · example
Advice is “here is what I would do”.
Coaching, the way I practise it, is “here is the decision in front of you, and the questions that make it sharper”.

If you want a consultant, say so. The two jobs get messy when they are sold as the same thing.
Boundary post · example
I do not coach acute anxiety, couples in crisis, or anyone looking for a diagnosis.

Those need a clinician. If that is you, I will say so and stop. The work I take is decisions, habits and the stories people tell about their work.
Very short hook · example
If your goal has no weekly behaviour, it is a mood.

Pick the behaviour first. The goal can wait a week.
Framework · example
When a client is stuck on a “should”, I use this sequence:

1. Who installed the should?
2. What does it cost to keep it?
3. What would you do if it were optional?
4. What is the smallest public test of that answer?

We do not skip to step 4. The first two questions do most of the work.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

What to skip

Do not invent client results, income figures or “I helped her 10x her business” stories. Do not give the impression that a post replaces therapy, clinical care or regulated advice. Do not argue with a stranger’s trauma in public. And do not open with “DM me if you want this for yourself”; if you offer a call, put the terms in a reply or a link in your bio, after the useful part.

FAQ

Can I tell a client story?

Only in a form that cannot identify the person, and only if your contract and professional rules allow it. When in doubt, write a clearly labelled fictional example, as we do on this page.

Is motivation ever worth posting?

A precise line can work as a hook. It is rarely enough on its own. Add a question, a step, or a distinction, or it is just decoration.

Will this fill my calendar?

It might start conversations. It might not. A writing habit is not a sales funnel by itself, and we do not claim otherwise.
