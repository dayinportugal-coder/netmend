X post ideas for dentists

Dental posts that work in public are usually explanations, not before-and-after marketing. People ask questions about pain, waiting, what a treatment actually involves, and how to avoid an emergency. They ignore slogans, and they should ignore any post that promises a perfect smile on a deadline.

This page is for dentists, hygienists and clinic managers writing for a general audience. It is not clinical advice, and it is not a substitute for an examination. Do not post radiographs, clinical photos or stories that could identify a patient. Do not promise outcomes, speed or pain-free treatment you cannot guarantee. Follow the advertising rules that apply to you in your country.

The useful pattern is: name the situation, say what you usually look for, say when someone should call, and stop there. Leave room for “this is general information; your own teeth need their own exam.”

Ideas that stay on the right side of the rules

  • A plain-language explanation of a common procedure (a filling, a hygienist visit, a root canal in outline), with what the patient will be asked to do afterwards.
  • A habit post: how long to wait after a child has a sugary drink, why night guards are fitted, what “sensitive to cold” can mean — without diagnosing a stranger.
  • How your clinic runs: emergency slots, late-cancellation rules, what to bring, whether you accept a particular plan — facts, not pressure.
  • A myth you still hear, corrected in one paragraph, with a note that cases differ.
  • Prevention that is specific, not “brush twice a day” on its own: interdental brushes around a bridge, fluoride toothpaste and a pea-sized amount for children, when a sports mouthguard is worth it.
  • A signpost. Swelling, a knocked-out tooth, uncontrolled bleeding: tell people to seek urgent care, not to wait for your content.

Example posts you can adapt

Concrete-fact hook · example
If a permanent tooth is knocked out, the advice we give on the phone is: pick it up by the crown, not the root, rinse it briefly with milk or saline if it is dirty, and try to put it back in the socket. If you cannot, keep it in milk and get to urgent dental care immediately.

This is general first aid, not a treatment plan. Time matters.
Question + list · example
Before a child’s first filling, parents usually ask me these three things:

1. Will they need local anaesthetic? Often yes, and we explain how it feels.
2. Can they eat afterwards? Soft food until the numbness has gone, so they do not bite their lip.
3. Will it look silver? That depends on the material we agree with you.

None of this replaces the conversation in the chair.
Short twist · example
“I only came because it stopped hurting” is a sentence I hear too often.

Pain that disappears is not always a problem that has disappeared. If a tooth was severe and then went quiet, it is worth an exam, not a wait-and-see on social media.
Clinic process · example
We keep two emergency slots on weekday mornings for regular patients with acute pain or swelling.

Call, do not post. If those slots have gone, we will tell you whether to wait, come to the afternoon, or go to urgent care. A message on X is not a booking.
Myth correction · example
Bleeding gums are not “just because you brush too hard” by default.

Sometimes the brushing is too abrasive. Sometimes it is inflammation. The only honest next step is to have a look, not to switch toothbrushes on a guess from a post.
Habit post · example
If you have a bridge or tight contacts, floss alone may not be enough.

Ask your hygienist to show you an interdental brush size that actually fits. The size matters; a brush that does not pass the contact does no work.
Very short hook · example
A knocked-out adult tooth is an urgent problem, not a morning-after problem.

Milk, do not let the root dry, and get to urgent dental care.
Explanation · example
A routine hygienist visit here is not “just a polish”.

We look at gums, plaque, and any sites that bleed, then clean what we can see and feel, and we will tell you if something needs a dentist. It is not a cosmetic extra we added for the sake of it.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

What to skip

No patient photos, even with a blur, unless you have proper consent and it is allowed. No “smile makeover in a week” promises. No diagnosing a stranger from a comment. No invented five-star stories. FAQ

Can we post reviews?

Only real ones, with the permission and wording your regulator and the review site allow. Never write a review yourself and pretend a patient wrote it.

Is X a good place for appointment reminders?

No. Bookings, medical history and reminders belong in your proper clinic channels. Use X for general education and public information about how the clinic works.

Who should approve these posts?

A registered clinician in the practice, not only the marketing person. If a draft over-promises, cut it.
