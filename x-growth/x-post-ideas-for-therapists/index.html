X post ideas for therapists

Therapy does not happen on X. What can happen, carefully, is psychoeducation: a plain explanation of a term, a description of how you work, or a signpost to proper help. Anything that looks like a diagnosis, a crisis intervention, or a recognisable client story does not belong here.

This page is for therapists, counsellors and similar practitioners writing in public. It is not therapy, not a clinical guideline, and not a way to take someone on mid-crisis. Follow your professional body’s advertising and confidentiality rules. Do not give the impression that a post can replace care. If someone is in immediate danger, they need emergency services or a local crisis route, not a thread.

Write generally, invite people to use your proper booking or enquiry channel, and keep the comments from becoming a group session you cannot supervise.

What is usually appropriate

  • A plain-language explanation of a term you actually use (a type of session, a boundary, a commonly misunderstood word), with a note that meanings vary.
  • How you work: length of sessions, in-person or online, who you usually see, how to enquire, fees if you publish them.
  • A boundary post: you do not provide therapy in direct messages or comments.
  • A signpost to official or established crisis and directory resources in your country, not to a random thread.
  • A myth correction that does not mock people who believed it.
  • What you do not treat, or when you refer on, if you can say that clearly.

Example posts you can adapt

Concrete-fact hook · example
I do not provide therapy in direct messages or in the comments of a post.

If you want to enquire about a session, use the contact form on my site, which goes into a confidential inbox. If you are in immediate danger, contact emergency services or a local crisis line in your country. A social media account is not a clinic.
Question + list · example
People often ask what “a 50-minute session” actually contains. In my practice, it usually looks like this:

1. A short check-in about the week.
2. The work you want to bring that day.
3. A few minutes at the end to notice what is staying with you, and practicalities (the next date, fees).

Other practitioners work differently. This is a description of my room, not a rule for therapy.
Short twist · example
Feeling better after one conversation is possible, and it is not the same thing as finished work.

I will not sell a “breakthrough in a thread”. If we work together, we will talk about pace in the room, not on here.
How I work · example
I am a counsellor working with adults, online, in English, usually weekly, at £70 for 50 minutes. In a live bio I would also name the professional register I actually sit on — I am not inventing one here.

I am not taking on emergency or crisis work through this account. The enquiry form is on the site; I will reply there, not in a public thread.
Term, carefully · example
“Burnout” is a word people use for many different states: exhaustion, boredom, depression, a bad job, or something clinical that needs a different kind of help.

I will not tell you which one you have from a post. If you are struggling, a first step is to speak to a qualified practitioner or your doctor, not to diagnose yourself from a thread.
Referral / limit · example
If what you need is specialist treatment I do not offer, I will say so and, where I can, point you to a directory or a service that does.

That is part of the job. It is not a rejection of you as a person.
Very short hook · example
A comment section is not a therapy group.

I will not go into your history there, and I will ask others not to either. Use a proper, confidential channel.
Myth correction · example
Therapy is not “advice from a wise friend”, at least not the way I practise it.

It is a structured, confidential relationship with a frame (time, fee, consent). If you want advice only, a friend or a coach may be a better fit, and it is fair to say that.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

What to skip

No client vignettes that could be recognised, no diagnosing a public figure, no “my client today”, no crisis therapy in a reply, no miracle claims. Have a supervisor or a trusted colleague read a draft if you are unsure. The free writer can help with plain language; it cannot hold clinical responsibility.

FAQ

Can I share an anonymised story?

Many professional bodies warn against this, because anonymity fails more easily than you think. A clearly labelled fictional example, or no story at all, is safer.

Should I turn comments off?

Sometimes, especially under a crisis-related post. You cannot moderate a comment section as if it were a clinic.

Is it ethical to have a fee post?

Publishing fees is often fair and reduces wasted enquiries. Keep it factual. Do not pressure.
