How to grow on X as a beginner

If you are starting from a small or empty account, the job is not to “hack the algorithm”. It is to be findable and worth reading: a clear bio, a few posts that show how you think, and replies in conversations you actually know something about. Growth, if it comes, is a side-effect.

There is no honest timeline for this, and we will not invent one. Accounts grow at very different speeds depending on the topic, the luck of a single post, and whether the person already has an audience elsewhere. Anyone selling you a follower package, a “growth pod”, or a guaranteed schedule is selling you a problem.

Do the simple work consistently for a month before you judge the channel. If after that you still hate it, you are allowed to stop.

A beginner path that does not rely on tricks

  • Fix the profile first. A name people can search, a bio that names the job and who it is for, a link that works, and a pin that still describes you. See the bio examples on this hub.
  • Write five to ten posts that could live for a month: a method, a process, a lesson from work, a definition, a limit of what you do. These are the posts a new visitor will see.
  • Pick a pace you can keep. Three good posts a week will beat a seven-day burst and a silence. There is no required daily quota.
  • Spend a short, fixed time on replies — 15–20 minutes — in topics you know. Add a fact, a follow-up, or a reasoned disagreement. Do not pitch.
  • Talk to people, not at “the algorithm”. Follow a modest number of people whose work you actually read. You do not need to follow thousands, and you should not mass-follow.
  • Ignore the usual traps: bought followers, engagement pods, giveaway-for-a-follow schemes, and tools that post bait on your behalf.

Example first posts for a new account

Who I am, without a slogan · example
I design five-page marketing sites for small B2B firms, on a fixed-scope proposal, with two rounds of revisions.

I will write here about briefs, scope, and the work itself. If you need a “vision workshop” with no deliverable, I am the wrong person.
A method · example
I will not start a project without a four-line brief: what already exists, what “done” looks like in files, the real date, and one person who can sign off.

If those lines cannot be written, we do not book a kickoff call.
A limit · example
I do not give personal tax advice on this account.

I will post general notes about records and how our firm works, in England and Wales, and I will point you to the official pages when I cite a date. Enquiries go to the firm’s email, in the link.
A lesson from work · example
Three jobs that went wrong last year had the same first week: a call, no written brief, and “we will know it when we see it”.

I now send the brief back for a yes before I open the file.
A reply, as a beginner · example
Under a post about onboarding: “We stalled in the same place — people did not know whether to add a client or an invoice first. We hid the other buttons until one client existed. Not a universal fix, but it was the right one for that screen.”

No link. No “I help with onboarding”.
What not to lead with · example
“New here! Please follow me, I post about hustle, mindset and 10x growth every day.”

This tells a stranger almost nothing, and it asks for a favour before you have been useful. Write one useful post first. The follow is their decision.
A quiet weekly cadence · example
A sustainable week, for many people, looks like this:

- One process or method post.
- One example or lesson from work.
- One short observation, or a reply-heavy day with no new post.

Adjust it. The point is a pace you still keep in week four.
Very short hook · example
You do not need a growth hack to start on X.

You need a clear bio, a handful of posts you are still happy with in a month, and replies you would also stand behind if they were screenshotted.

FAQ

How many followers do I need before it is “working”?

There is no threshold. A hundred people who care about your trade are more useful than a thousand who were bought or baited. Look at whether the replies are serious, not only at the count.

Do I need to post every day?

No. A pace you can keep, with posts you would still sign in a month, is enough to start. Daily posting is a choice, not a rule.

Should I tell people I am new?

You can, once, if it is true and light. Do not make “new here, please welcome me” the pin. Let the work introduce you.
