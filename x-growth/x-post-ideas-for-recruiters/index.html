X post ideas for recruiters

“Exciting opportunity, DM me your CV” is why people mute recruiters. The posts that get serious candidates usually describe the work, the constraints, the location or pattern of work, and a salary range you can defend.

This is for in-house and agency recruiters. Only post roles you are actually mandated to fill. Do not invent companies, salary bands or visa sponsorship. Do not share a candidate’s details in public. Be honest if a role is on-site, if the budget is fixed, or if the process has four interviews.

One well-written role post is more useful than ten identical “we are hiring” lines. Between roles, write about how you interview, what a good brief looks like, and what you will not do.

How to post a role, and what else to post

  • A role with constraints: title, team, location/remote pattern, salary range, must-have skills, and how to apply (not only “DM me”).
  • A process post: number of stages, whether there is a task, typical time from first chat to offer — if you actually know it.
  • A briefing post for hiring managers (what you need from them), which also signals to candidates how you work.
  • A market observation you can source — or skip the number if you cannot.
  • What you will not do: bait-and-switch salaries, unpaid “trials” that are real work, ghosting after a final stage if you can help it.
  • A closed-role note. When it is filled or pulled, say so, so people stop writing.

Example posts you can adapt

Concrete-fact hook · example
We are hiring a product designer, permanent, for a 12-person team in Manchester, hybrid Tuesday and Wednesday in the office.

Salary: £55,000–£65,000, plus the standard company pension. You will own the design of the billing settings, not a pitch deck. Apply through the link in the first reply; a portfolio of real product work is enough. No free trial task that looks like production work.
Question + list · example
If you want me to recruit a role, I need these four things in writing before I go to market:

1. A salary range you will actually offer.
2. Must-haves vs nice-to-haves, in skills, not in years.
3. The interview process, including any task, and who is on each stage.
4. Whether visa sponsorship is possible.

Without those, I will not post the job.
Short twist · example
“Competitive salary” is not information.

A range is information. If the range is tight, say so. Candidates will find out at offer; I would rather they decide earlier.
Process · example
For this role the process is: a 30-minute call with me, a 60-minute conversation with the hiring manager, a paid design exercise (half a day, £250), then a final conversation with two peers.

We aim to do all of that in three weeks. If we slip, I will tell you.
Closed role · example
The product designer role I posted on 28 October is now at offer stage, so I am closing public applications.

Thank you if you wrote in. If you interviewed and you have not heard, you will hear this week, including if the answer is no.
What I will not do · example
I will not send your CV to a client without your say-so, and I will not take a brief that uses “rockstar” as a substitute for a salary band.

If that is the brief, I will send it back.
Very short hook · example
If the job post has no location, no pattern of work and no salary range, it is not finished.

I will not share it until those lines are on it.
Candidate care · example
If you reached a final stage with us and we hired someone else, I will tell you, and I will tell you once, clearly, not with a vague “we went in another direction” and silence.

I do not always get this right when the week explodes. It is still the standard.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

What to skip

No scraped CV comments, no “I have a role that would be perfect for you” in a public reply to a stranger’s personal post, no fake urgency. Use the free writer to draft the role post, then check the salary, the location and the link before it goes out.

FAQ

Should every role be a thread?

Only if you have more than a post’s worth of real information (process, team, stack). Otherwise one clear post plus a link is enough.

Can I post roles in several time zones as “remote”?

Say the time zone or the countries you will actually hire in. “Remote” that means “UK hours only” should say so.

Will this fill a pipeline by itself?

It can help, especially for visible roles. Most mandates still need direct search. Do not promise a founder otherwise.
