X post ideas for marketing agencies

A wall of award badges and “we make brands unforgettable” does not help a buyer brief you. Agencies that write well on X usually talk about a constraint, a piece of work, how they measure, or what they will not take on.

This is for small and mid-size agencies (content, performance, brand, SEO, social). If you quote a result, use a real figure the client has cleared, with the time period and what else was going on. “3x growth” with no baseline is an advert, not a case. Do not share a client’s unreleased work.

Between case posts, write about how you start, how you report, and the brief you wish you received more often.

Posts that a buyer can use

  • A piece of work with the constraint: budget, time, channel, what was off-limits.
  • A measurement note: what you actually report, and what you will not claim credit for.
  • A briefing checklist you wish clients arrived with.
  • A process post: discovery, first 30 days, monthly reporting, who is in the Slack.
  • A disagreement with a fad, with your method instead of a dunk.
  • What you will not sell (guaranteed rankings, fake reviews, a 10-post “viral” package).

Example posts you can adapt

Concrete-fact hook · example
For a 12-week search project we shipped last spring, the constraint was: no new domain, no paid ads, and the product pages could not be redesigned.

We rewrote 14 titles and meta descriptions, fixed the internal links on the help centre, and cut two overlapping landing pages. Organic sign-ups from those 14 pages went from 110 a month to 164 a month, in the 90 days after launch, with no other campaign on them. The client has agreed to those numbers being public. That is the whole case.
Question + list · example
If you are briefing an agency, these five lines save a week:

1. The business outcome (not “more awareness”).
2. What “good” looks like in a number you already track.
3. What is off-limits (tone, channels, claims).
4. Who signs off, and how often they are available.
5. The budget, as a range if necessary.

We will still have questions. We will have fewer.
Short twist · example
We will not sell you a “viral campaign”.

We can write work that is easy to share, and we can tell you what happened after it ran. Nobody honest can sell virality as a deliverable. If an agency promises it, ask how they will refund you when it does not happen.
Measurement · example
We do not take credit for a revenue spike if the client also ran a TV ad or a price cut in the same week.

Our monthly report says what we did, what moved, and what else was in the market. It is less exciting than a vanity dashboard. It is harder to argue with six months later.
Process · example
The first 30 days with us are: a written kickoff, access to the accounts we need, a measurement plan you approve, and one small ship (a page, an email, a test), not a 90-page strategy with no output.

If we cannot ship something small in 30 days, we will say why, early.
What we will not take · example
We do not run campaigns that need fake reviews, hidden affiliate posts, or claims the product cannot stand behind.

That is not a moral speech. It is a risk we are not willing to put our name on.
Very short hook · example
If the only KPI is “engagement”, the brief is not finished.

Tell us which business number this work is for, even if the number is a leading one.
Work in public, carefully · example
We rewrote the onboarding email sequence for a B2B tool. The constraint was: no new design, three emails, and no invented testimonials.

The change was structural: one job per email, the price in email two, a human name on the last line. We will not post a lift figure for this one; the client asked us not to. The method is still fair to share.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

What to skip

No award bragging without the work. No “we grew a brand 10x” with no date. No subtweet wars with other agencies. Draft with the free writer if you want; have the account lead check every number and every client name.

FAQ

Should we post every campaign?

Only with clearance, and only if you can describe the constraint and the result honestly. A maybe-metric is worse than no case.

Is thought leadership dead?

Vague thought leadership is. A specific method, a disagreement, or a briefing post is still useful.

Can we ask for a follow?

You can. It is a weak close after a good post. Put the site or the mailing list in the bio and let the work do the next step.
