How to write X posts in your own voice

Voice, on X, is not a brand adjective. It is the pattern in your sentences: how long they are, whether you use jokes, how formal you are, whether you say “I” or “we”, whether you hedge. The fastest way to lose it is to paste a topic into a generic tool and publish the polished, nobody-in-particular result.

The way back is simple, and slightly slower: start from words you have already written, then draft, then cut anything you would not say out loud to a sharp peer. A tool can help with structure and with volume. It cannot be you, and it should not be allowed to invent a figure or a softness you do not use.

A practical method

  • Collect 5–15 posts, emails or paragraphs you already like. Prefer ones real people have already seen. Include a reply or a tight explanation, not only announcements.
  • Name three traits, privately: for example “short sentences, dry, no emoji”, or “warmer, I use ‘we’, I explain the why”. This is a check, not a manifesto.
  • Write a messy first pass of the new idea, in the words you would actually use, even if it is ugly.
  • Only then use a tool or a structure (a hook formula, a thread shape, or the X Post Writer) to get variations.
  • Read each draft out loud. Cut the adjectives you never say, the false intimacy, the fake number, the “excited to announce”, the Americanism you do not use, or the formality you do not use.
  • Keep one draft. Publishing four near-duplicates in a day does not sound like a voice. It sounds like a machine.

The same fact, three voices (all fictional)

Drier, shorter · example
We hid every button on the home screen until the user adds one client.

People were stalling on “what do I do first?”. This is the fix we are testing. I will post if the drop-off moves, not before.
Warmer, still specific · example
The most common first-session question we saw was “do I add a client or an invoice first?” — and we had given them both buttons, like that was helpful.

We now ask you to add a client, and we hide the rest until you have. If you try it and it feels worse, I want to hear it.
More formal · example
We changed the empty state of the workspace. Until one client record exists, other actions are hidden.

The aim is to remove an ambiguous first step that showed up repeatedly in trial drop-off. I will report the result when we have two weeks of data.
A voice you should not borrow · example
“Folks, I am SUPER excited to share that we just 10x’d onboarding by leveraging a delightful empty-state journey!! 🚀”

If you do not talk like this, do not let a tool talk like this in your name. Also, “10x’d” needs a baseline you probably do not have in this sentence.
Where voice actually lives · example
It lives in: sentence length, hedging (“I think”, “usually”), humour, whether you name the messy part, whether you use contractions, whether you say “I” or hide behind “one”.

It does not live in a list of brand values. If those values never appear in your ordinary email, they will not appear, honestly, in a post.
Editing pass · example
Read the draft next to one of your old posts.
If the new one is suddenly full of “unlock”, “journey”, “game-changer”, or a statistic you did not type, cut.
If the new one is suddenly stiffer than you ever are, cut the starch, not the fact.
When you write in public for a firm · example
Agree, in writing if you can, what “we” may say: no client names, no unofficial legal positions, no unauthorised financial promotions.

Your personal sharpness can still show, inside that fence. The fence is not the same thing as sounding like a brochure.
Very short hook · example
If you would not say it to a sharp peer, do not post it.

That rule removes most of the fake voice problems on its own.

FAQ

What if I do not have old posts yet?

Use emails, a proposal, a README, a lesson you already taught. The sample only has to be writing that already sounds like you. If you have none, write three short posts by hand first, then start sampling those.

Can I be two voices, personal and professional?

Yes, if you are clear which account is which, and you do not mix them in a way that surprises a client or a regulator. Each account still needs a consistent sample.

Why 10 drafts, not one?

Because the first draft a model produces is often the most generic. Looking at ten, then keeping one you would actually say, is the point of the tool — not publishing ten.
