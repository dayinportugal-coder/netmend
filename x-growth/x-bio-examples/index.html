X bio examples

A bio has a short, unforgiving job: tell a stranger who you help, what you do, and where to go next. Jokes, slogan adjectives and fake metrics waste that space. You can be dry and still be memorable if the line is specific.

You do not need a hook worthy of a billboard. You need one sentence a sharp stranger would not have to reread, plus a way to go further (a site, a shop, a booking page). If you are on a personal account and a professional one, say which this is. Do not invent a title, a credential, a follower count, or a “featured in” line.

Names and handles are not part of the examples below. The structure is. Swap in your real facts, and nothing else.

What usually belongs in a bio

  • Who you help, or the job you do, in words a stranger uses. “I write X posts for dentists” is clearer than “storyteller / visionary”.
  • A constraint or a place if it matters: a city, a jurisdiction, “adults only”, “UK tax”, “no medical advice here”.
  • One proof that is real (a book title, a product name, a registration you actually hold). Skip it if you would have to inflate it.
  • A link. The bio link should go where you actually want the click: the site, the product, the booking form. Not a pile of five unlabelled links if you can help it.
  • What this account is not, when that prevents harm: “Not legal advice”, “Not a clinic”, “I do not take job pitches in DMs”.

Example bios, with a note on why

SaaS founder · example
Plain-language invoicing for sole traders who already have a bank CSV. I write about what we ship, and what we will not build.

Why it works: the user and the file are named. There is no “revolutionising finance”.
Freelance designer · example
Brand and web design for small B2B firms. Five-page marketing sites, fixed-scope proposals, two rounds of revisions. Portfolio in the link. No “quick calls” without a brief.

Why it works: the package and a boundary are visible before a DM.
Dentist / clinic (general info) · example
General dental practice in Leeds. This account is for clinic hours, plain-language explanations, and public information. Appointments: use the practice number or the form on the site, not a DM. Not a diagnosis.

Why it works: it sets the channel and the limit.
Accountant · example
Chartered accountant, England and Wales. General posts about records, deadlines and how we work. Not personal tax advice. Enquiries: the firm’s email in the link.

Why it works: credential, limit, and a proper channel.
Author · example
Novels and essays. Next book: The Quiet Harbour, 3 March. Process notes are here; the buy link is in the site. I do not review unpublished manuscripts in DMs.

Why it works: the dated fact, the link job, and a boundary.
Recruiter · example
In-house recruiter, product and design roles, Manchester, hybrid two days a week. I post live roles with a salary range, or I do not post them. Applications: the official form, not a DM of a CV screenshot.

Why it works: the mandate and the process are clear.
Therapist (careful) · example
Counsellor for adults, online. In a live bio, name the register you actually belong to; this example does not invent one. This is not a therapy service and not a crisis line. Enquiries through the contact form only. If you are in immediate danger, contact emergency services.

Why it works: it prevents the account being mistaken for a clinic.
A bio to avoid · example
“CEO | Investor | Speaker | Thought leader | 10x | Husband | Opinions my own | DM for collabs | 🚀”

This is a pile of titles, a fake-flex, and no reader. If you need a personal line, one true one is enough: the job, the city, or the product.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

Display name, picture, and the first posts

Use a name people can say and search for. A face or a clear mark is easier to recognise than a dark, cropped logo, but that is your choice; some professions prefer a mark. The first visible posts should match the bio. If the bio says you write about shipping a product, the pin should not be a year-old joke.

FAQ

How long should a bio be?

Short enough to read on a phone without effort. One tight paragraph, or two very short ones, is enough. If you need a CV, that belongs on the site.

Should I put a price in the bio?

If the price is stable and you want that filter, yes. If it varies, describe how you work and put figures on the site.

Can I joke?

A small joke is fine if the job is still clear. If the joke needs explaining, or if it muddies a regulated role, cut it.
