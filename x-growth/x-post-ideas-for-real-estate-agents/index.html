X post ideas for real estate agents

A listing post that only says “just listed” is easy to scroll past. The posts that get saved and replied to on X are usually specific: a street, a constraint, a number you can defend, or a buyer mistake you see every month.

This page is for agents who already work a patch and want writing they can post without sounding like a brochure. It is not a lead-gen guarantee. Reach on X depends on the account, the timing and whether the post is actually useful. Use only addresses, prices and photos you have the right to publish, and never post a client’s private situation.

The pattern that works for most agents is narrow and factual. Name the street or the building type, add one constraint (stairs, flood zone, leasehold, parking, school catchment), and leave a clean question for people who already live nearby. Soft marketing — “dream home”, “won’t last”, “DM me for the link” as the first line — tends to be ignored, especially from a new account.

What to post instead of a slogan

  • A listing with one constraint. Price and beds are not enough. The useful line is the thing a buyer would otherwise only learn at the second viewing: the garden is north-facing, the kitchen is a galley, the service charge is £2,400 a year.
  • A street-level observation, not a market forecast. “Two family houses on this terrace have sold in 11 weeks” is useful if it is true and dated. “The market is on fire” is not.
  • A buyer or seller mistake you can explain in four lines. Offers subject to an unsold chain, skipping the survey, underestimating stamp duty, ignoring the lease length. No client names.
  • A walk-through detail. The cupboard that holds the boiler, the room that does not fit a double bed, the bus that actually runs on a Sunday. These are the posts neighbours reply to.
  • A process post. What happens between “offer accepted” and keys, in the order of your market, with typical time ranges you really see — and a note that every case is different.
  • Replies to local news. A planning decision, a new bus route, a school catchment change. Add a street-level take, not a reshare with “thoughts?”.

Example posts you can adapt

All eight are written for a fictional agent in a real-looking neighbourhood. Change every fact before you post.

Concrete-fact hook · example
This two-bed on Mapperley Road is £285,000, but the useful number is the service charge: £2,400 a year, and the lease has 82 years left.

Second-floor, no lift, one permit parking space in the next street. Floor plan is in the first reply.
Question + list · example
If you are buying a flat in this building, look at these three lines before you fall for the kitchen photo:

1. Lease length (this one: 82 years).
2. Service charge for the last 3 years, not just this year.
3. Whether the parking space is in the lease or a separate licence.

I put the documents in the listing pack. Ask for them.
Short twist · example
The “south-facing garden” on this listing is a 4-metre yard with a brick wall on the sunny side after 2pm.

Still a good flat if you want the park at the end of the road. Just not the garden in the photo crop.
Process / lessons · example
Three offers I saw accepted in this postcode last month, and what slowed them down:

1. Buyer’s own flat not yet on the market.
2. Survey flagged a flat roof; nobody had priced a repair.
3. Solicitor started on the leasehold pack a week late.

None of these are unusual. Budget time for them.
Walk-through detail · example
The second bedroom on Hartington Avenue takes a 140cm bed and a narrow wardrobe, not a double and a desk.

If you need a proper office, this is a one-bed with a box room. The living room, though, is the full width of the house.
Neighbourhood note · example
The 45 bus on Sunday mornings is every 30 minutes from the stop at the end of this street, first one at 07:12.

Useful if you actually have to get to the station, and easy to miss when the listing only says “close to transport”.
Very short hook · example
Do not skip the survey on a 1930s terrace because the kitchen is new.

The kitchen does not tell you about the roof, the lintels or the damp in the rear wall.
Reply-style post · example
A lot of first-time buyers in this area bid at the asking price and hope the chain is clean.

Ask, in writing: is the seller buying? Is that purchase agreed? If it is not, your timeline is their timeline, not yours.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

What to skip

Do not invent sold-price comparisons or “values have risen 12% on this street” unless you can point to a source and a date. Do not post interior photos of a property you do not have permission to show. Do not argue with other agents in public about a shared listing. And do not use the first line of a post to ask for a DM; put the invitation at the end, if you need it at all.

FAQ

Can I post a client’s house before it goes live on the portals?

Only if you have the seller’s permission, in the form your firm already uses, and you follow any portal or franchise rules you are under. When in doubt, wait until it is publicly listed, or post a detail that does not identify the house.

Should I post every new listing?

You can, but a feed of “just listed” photos with no constraint gets ignored. Rotate: one listing with a real constraint, one neighbourhood note, one process post. Quality beats a daily brochure.

Will these posts bring buyers?

Sometimes a useful post is forwarded to a buyer. Often it just makes neighbours and other agents take you more seriously. Nobody can honestly promise leads from a template, including us.
