X post ideas for interior designers

A handsome room with no caption is a mood board. Designers who get useful conversations on X usually write the brief: the constraint, the budget band if they can share it, why that floor, why that light, what the client refused, what you would still change.

Get permission before you post a private home, a child’s room, or a project a client has not signed off for publicity. Do not invent a budget or a brand collaboration. Do not pass off a supplier’s render as a finished room. Credit photographers, makers and architects when their work is in the frame.

The caption should answer “why is it like this?” rather than “look at this”.

What to write under a picture, and without one

  • The brief in one paragraph: who uses the room, the problem, the non-negotiables.
  • A single material or product decision and the reason (light, acoustics, wear, lead time).
  • A process post: survey, concept, samples, ordering, site, snagging — and who is responsible at each step.
  • A budget or fee note if you are comfortable: how you charge, what a small project starts at.
  • A supplier or maker credit that is specific.
  • A thing you would still change, which is more credible than a perfect reveal.

Example posts you can adapt

Concrete-fact hook · example
This living room had to work for a wheelchair user and two dogs, with no step at the terrace door.

We specified a durable, tight-weave wool, a 90cm clear route through the furniture, and a door threshold designed with the joiner for a level change of less than 5mm. Photographer: Maya Cole, used with the client’s permission. (Fictional credit, for the example.)
Question + list · example
Before I start a concept, I need these four things:

1. Who uses the room, at what times of day.
2. What must stay (a piano, a inherited table, a colour someone loves).
3. The budget for furniture and for works, even as a range.
4. The date someone actually needs to move back in.

A Pinterest board is useful. It is not a brief.
Short twist · example
The most expensive thing in this kitchen is not the worktop.

It is the six weeks of joinery, because the room was not square. I would rather tell you that at survey than surprise you at install.
Decision · example
We used a matt emulsion, not a washable “kitchen paint”, on this dining room ceiling, because the sheen was showing every ripple in a 19th-century slab.

The walls are a washable mid-sheen, because of the chairs. Two finishes, one colour. Not a slogan, a response to the light.
Process · example
A typical residential project with me runs: paid survey and a written proposal, a concept with samples, a specification and order list, then site visits during works and a snagging list at the end.

I do not order until the specification is signed. Lead times sit in that document, not in a hopeful chat.
Fee · example
For a single-room refresh (no structural works), I start at £2,400 for the design package: survey, concept, specification and a shopping list.

Purchasing, site visits and a full renovation are quoted separately. I would rather put this here than hide it until a call.
Very short hook · example
If the only brief is “make it look like the photo”, I will send it back.

Tell me how the room is used, and what the photo is getting right — the light, the storage, or just the colour.
Credit and limit · example
The table is by a local maker, Sam Reza, in oak, made to the width of the alcove. (Fictional maker, for the example.)

I will not call it a “bespoke lifestyle moment”. It is a table that fits. The client has agreed to this post; the maker is credited because the work is theirs.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

What to skip

No uncredited photographs, no “before” that humiliates a client, no fake brand trips. Draft captions with the free writer if you want, then check permissions, names and whether the room is finished enough to show.

FAQ

Can I post a project still on site?

Only with the client’s okay, and only if you are honest that it is in progress. A dusty room presented as a reveal helps no one.

Should I post every sample board?

Occasionally, if you can explain a choice. A weekly uncaptioned board is just a catalogue.

Do I need a photographer?

For a hero project, usually yes. Credit them. A phone photo is fine for a process or a material post if it is honest.
