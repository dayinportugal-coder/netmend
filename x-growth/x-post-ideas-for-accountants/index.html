X post ideas for accountants

Clients do not need a joke about tax season as much as they need a dated reminder, a list of records, or a clear picture of how you work. Accountants who write well on X stay general, name the jurisdiction, and refuse to do someone’s tax in the comments.

This is general information, not tax, audit or accounting advice. Rules change. Name the tax year and the country, link an official source when you cite a date or a threshold, and tell people to check their own position with their adviser. Do not post client data, not even as a “sanitised” screenshot.

The useful post is often a checklist or a process: what to keep, when to send it, what you will not file without. Humour is fine if it is kind and does not punch down at people who are scared of the paperwork.

Post types that help, and stay general

  • A dated deadline with the official name of the obligation and a link to the authority, plus “this may not be your date if your facts differ”.
  • A records list for a common situation (a first year of self-employment, a simple limited company, a rental property) without claiming it is complete for everyone.
  • How your firm works: what a bookkeeping pack looks like, when you need it, whether you work on a fixed fee for a named service.
  • A software or process note — bank feeds, receipt capture — as a method, not a sponsored claim unless it is one.
  • A myth correction (“I can put my whole holiday through the company”) without humiliating the person who asked.
  • A capacity post if you are closing the books for new clients this quarter, with what “complete records” means.

Example posts you can adapt

Concrete-fact hook · example
If you are a self-assessment taxpayer in the UK and you file online, the 31 January deadline is the one most people mean — but it is not the only date that matters, and it can be different if you have other obligations.

Check your own account on the official site, or ask your accountant. I will not confirm a stranger’s deadline in a reply.
Question + list · example
If this is your first year trading as a sole trader, these are the records I usually ask people to start keeping from day one:

1. Sales, with invoices or a simple sales list.
2. Purchases, with receipts.
3. Bank statements for the business account.
4. A note of money you put in or took out.

This is a starting list, not a complete set of rules for every trade. Ask your adviser.
Short twist · example
A shoebox of receipts in April is not a bookkeeping system.

A monthly export from your bank and a folder of invoices, even a rough one, will save you more money than a clever workaround.
Process · example
For year-end with us, we ask for the bookkeeping pack eight weeks before your filing date: bank statements, sales and purchase listings, payroll reports if you have them, and a note of anything unusual.

If the pack arrives late, we will tell you, early, whether the date is still realistic. We would rather have that conversation in week one than in week eight.
Myth correction · example
“I can run my personal shopping through the company if I take it out later” is a sentence that causes mess.

What you may or may not put through a company depends on the rules and on your facts. Treat personal and business spending as separate, and ask your accountant before you invent a method.
Capacity · example
We are not taking on new limited-company clients for a 31 January filing this close to the date.

If you already have complete records and you need a name for next year, write to the practice with a short description of the business. A public reply on X is not a client acceptance.
Very short hook · example
If you cannot find last year’s accounts, start with the official filing record and your bank statements, not with a spreadsheet you might have made.

Then call your accountant. Guessing the numbers is how errors get filed.
Software note · example
A bank feed is useful, and it is not the same thing as a reviewed set of books.

Someone still has to match transfers, pick up missing invoices and ask you about the odd payments. That is the work we mean when we say “bookkeeping”, not the fact that the bank is connected.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

What to skip

No client screenshots, no “here is how I would file your situation”, no made-up savings figures, no joking about evading tax. Have a qualified person in the firm read drafts that mention dates or thresholds. The free writer can help you say it more clearly; it does not know this year’s rules.

FAQ

Can I quote a threshold or a rate?

Yes if you take it from an official source, date it, and warn that it may not apply to the reader. Do not quote a rate from memory if you would not put it in a letter.

Is a weekly tax tip worth it?

If it is accurate and general, yes. A wrong tip is worse than silence. A short process post ages better than a number that will change.

Should I argue with bad tax advice on X?

You can point to the official page, once, calmly. A long fight rarely helps the people you actually act for.
