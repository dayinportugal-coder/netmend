X post ideas for course creators

A countdown timer and a testimonial you wrote yourself will eventually be noticed. Course creators who get useful attention on X usually teach a small piece in public, describe who the course is not for, and talk about the syllabus as a set of outcomes, not a vibe.

This is for people who sell a class, a cohort or a self-paced course. Do not invent student results or enrolments. Do not fake a closing cart. If you quote a student, use their words, with permission. Be clear about price, duration, refunds and how much of your time is included.

The public lesson should be complete in itself — a method a reader can use today — not a teaser that stops just before the useful step.

Posts that teach, and posts that sell without tricks

  • A complete micro-lesson from the course, with the step people usually skip.
  • The syllabus as outcomes, not module titles. What someone will be able to do.
  • Who it is not for, in specific terms.
  • How the course is run: start dates, live vs recorded, how you give feedback, what happens after week four.
  • A change you made after the last cohort (a rewritten lesson, a dropped module).
  • Price and refunds as facts, when you are selling, not as a dark-pattern countdown.

Example posts you can adapt

Concrete-fact hook · example
A micro-lesson from the research module: before you interview anyone, write the decision the research is meant to change, in one sentence.

If you cannot write the decision, you are collecting quotes for a slide, not doing research. This is the whole lesson; there is no secret step behind a cart.
Question + list · example
This course is not for you if:

1. You need a certificate for an employer (I do not issue one).
2. You cannot spend four hours a week for six weeks.
3. You want me to do the interviews for you.
4. You are looking for a job in my company.

If none of those apply, the syllabus is on the site.
Short twist · example
I cut a module from the next cohort.

It was popular, and it did not change what people shipped. The time now goes to a second live critique. I would rather be useful than complete.
Syllabus as outcomes · example
By the end of week 6, you should be able to: write a one-page research plan, run five interviews, and present a decision memo with a recommendation and the evidence.

You will not leave with a personal brand, a Notion aesthetic, or a promise of a job. The memo is the product.
How it runs · example
The next cohort starts on 12 January, runs for six weeks, and is capped at 20 people so I can comment on the homework.

There is one live session a week, recorded. The price is $390, or two payments of $200. A full refund is available in the first 7 days, before week 2’s session. The page has the official wording.
Student quote, careful · example
After the last cohort, a student wrote (and agreed to be quoted): “I finally sent the memo. The interviews were messy, and I still sent it.”

That is the kind of result I am comfortable repeating. I will not invent a promotion or a salary for a testimonial.
Very short hook · example
If the sales page talks more about lifestyle than about the work you will do, I would not buy the course.

I try to write the opposite kind of page. Call me on it if I slip.
Lesson people skip · example
In the writing week, the step people skip is reading the memo out loud.

If you cannot say a sentence in one breath, the sentence is too long for a busy reader. This takes four minutes and saves a rewrite.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

What to skip

No fake “doors close at midnight” if they do not, no “I made my student $100k”, no tag-bombing. Use the free writer for lesson posts and syllabus copy, then check dates, prices and refund wording against the sales page.

FAQ

Should I give the best material away?

A complete small lesson is marketing and a filter. People who want the feedback, the structure and the deadlines still buy. Teasers that withhold the useful step annoy the people you want.

How often should I sell?

When a cohort is actually open, say so clearly. Between cohorts, teach. A permanent fake launch is easy to see.

Can I post student work?

With permission, and without exposing their employer or their data.
