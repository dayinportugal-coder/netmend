X post ideas for ecommerce founders

A shop account that only posts discount codes trains people to wait for a code. Founders who get useful attention usually post the product constraint, a shipping fact, a restock date, or a decision about packaging, returns or a supplier.

This is for independent shops and small ecommerce brands. If you quote a sales number, it should be real. If you show a review, it should be a real customer who agreed to be quoted. Follow the advertising and consumer rules that apply to you (including how you talk about discounts, stock and delivery times).

The useful post often answers a pre-purchase question: when it ships, what the fabric does, how a return works, why a colour is delayed.

Posts that help a shop, not just a sale

  • A product fact that is not in the first line of the product page: shrink, fit, origin, a limit of the material.
  • A shipping or customs note that is current, especially at peak season.
  • A restock or a discontinuation, with a date or a “we will not make this colour again”.
  • A returns or repairs policy in plain language.
  • A supplier or workshop note you can stand behind, without a fairy-tale origin story.
  • A decision: why you will not rush a pre-order, why you raised a price, why a SKU is gone.

Example posts you can adapt

Concrete-fact hook · example
The navy merino jumper shrinks if you tumble it. We say so on the care label and on the product page, and I am saying it here too.

Cold wash, flat dry. If you want a machine-tough knit, the cotton crew is the one.
Question + list · example
If you are ordering from us for a mid-December delivery in the UK, these are the cut-offs as they stand today:

1. Standard post: order by 16 December.
2. Next-day (weekdays): order by 3pm on 22 December.
3. We do not offer guaranteed 24 December delivery.

If the courier changes this, we will update the page and this account. Check the checkout, not an old post.
Short twist · example
We are not putting this bag back into production in yellow.

The dye lot we trusted is gone, and the substitute was a different colour in daylight. Better to retire it than to pretend it is the same bag.
Returns · example
You have 30 days from delivery to return an unused item with its tags, in the official returns bag, for a refund to the original payment method.

Sale items are included. Made-to-order belts are not. The full wording is on the returns page; this post is the short version, not a rewrite of the terms.
Supplier note · example
The leather for the belts comes from a tannery we have visited, in the same region as the workshop that cuts them.

I will not call it “sustainable” as a slogan. I will tell you where it is made and what it is made of. The workshop address is on the product page.
Price decision · example
The canvas tote goes from £28 to £32 on 1 December.

The cloth went up, and I would rather change the price than change the cloth. Orders placed before that date are at £28. I am saying it now so it is not a surprise.
Very short hook · example
If the estimated ship date in checkout says 10 days, it is 10 days.

We do not “leave a little extra” in the estimate. If we are late, we write to you.
Restock · example
The size 8 of the black ankle boot is back, 40 pairs, arriving on Thursday and going on the site when they are on the shelf.

There is no waitlist email this time; the last one was more noise than it was worth. If you want them, Thursday.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

What to skip

No fake urgency, no invented “selling out”, no purchased reviews, no before-and-after that is just a filter. Draft product and shipping posts with the free writer if you want, then check every date and price against the shop.

FAQ

Should I post every new SKU?

Post the ones where you have something to say: a fit note, a limit, a story about the material. A catalogue dump is what the shop is for.

Can I run a discount from X?

Yes if the code works, the dates are true, and the terms are clear. A code that fails is worse than no code.

Do I need a separate brand account?

If the founder’s voice and the shop’s voice are different, maybe. If you are small, one honest account is enough. Do not split your attention for the sake of it.
