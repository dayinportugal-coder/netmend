X post ideas for financial advisors

People look to advisors on X for definitions and process: what a pension wrapper is, how an annual review works, what “advice” means in your jurisdiction. They should not receive a personal recommendation in a public thread.

This is general information, not personal financial advice. Do not recommend a product, a stock, a crypto asset or a withdrawal in public. Do not promise returns. Do not use a client’s situation, even anonymised, if it could still be recognised. Follow your regulator’s marketing and promotion rules. If you are not allowed to promote certain products, do not.

The useful post explains a term, a document, or a step in how you work, then stops. “This depends on your own circumstances; take regulated advice before you act” is not decoration. It is the point.

What is usually appropriate to post

  • A definition (ISA vs general account, a workplace pension vs a personal one — in your country), dated, with an official source where a rule is cited.
  • How a first meeting works at your firm, what you will ask for, what you charge or how you are paid, if you are allowed and willing to say it.
  • A document checklist for a planning conversation (not a solicitation to hand over data on X).
  • A reminder to review named life events (a child, a house, a job change) without a product push.
  • A correction of a common mix-up, calmly, without dunking.
  • What you do not do (day trading tips, crypto calls, “get me 12%”).

Example posts you can adapt

Concrete-fact hook · example
A workplace pension and a personal pension are not interchangeable just because both have the word “pension” in the name.

Charges, investment choices, employer contributions and how you take money later can all differ. This is a general distinction, not advice on your pots. If you are transferring anything, get regulated advice or official guidance first.
Question + list · example
For a first planning meeting, I usually ask people to bring or have to hand:

1. Recent pension and investment statements.
2. A list of debts and the interest rates, if they know them.
3. Any protection policies and the cover amounts.
4. A rough picture of income and essential spending.

Send those through the firm’s proper channel, not in a direct message on X.
Short twist · example
A high past return is not a plan.

A plan, the way I use the word, is a set of actions and reviews that still make sense if the next decade is dull. I will not tell a stranger on here what that plan is for them.
How we work · example
We are a regulated firm. We charge a disclosed fee for advice, which we will put in writing before you agree to anything.

We do not give product recommendations on this account. If you want to talk, use the contact path on our website so we can do the checks we are required to do.
Life event · example
A new job is a sensible moment to look at the pension you are leaving and the one you are joining.

Look at charges, contributions, and whether you need regulated advice before you move anything. I will not tell you, in a comment, to transfer or to stay. The official guidance services in this country exist for a reason.
What I will not do · example
I will not comment on a stock, a coin, or a “hot” product on this account.

I will also not look at a screenshot of your portfolio in a public reply and tell you what to sell. That would not be advice worth having, and it would not be allowed.
Very short hook · example
If someone promises you a return, that is a sales line, not a plan.

Ask how they are paid, and what happens if the number is wrong.
Definition · example
“Emergency fund” is a useful idea and a sloppy phrase.

I use it to mean cash you can reach without selling a long-term investment, sized to your own essential spending. How many months is a personal question. I will not give you a number in a thread.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

What to skip

No performance charts as marketing unless your rules allow them and they are fair, clear and not misleading. No “clients like you made 8%”. No tips. Have compliance read anything that might be a financial promotion. The free writer can tidy language; it cannot make an unauthorised promotion legal.

FAQ

Can I talk about the market?

You can talk in general terms if your rules allow it. You cannot turn a market comment into a personal recommendation. When in doubt, say less and link official explanations.

May I use a client story?

Only with permission, only if it cannot identify them, and only if it is fair and not misleading. Many firms simply do not do this. That is a reasonable policy.

Is X a suitable advice channel?

No. It is a publishing channel. Advice belongs in your regulated process.
