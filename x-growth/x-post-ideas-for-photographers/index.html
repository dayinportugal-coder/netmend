X post ideas for photographers

A beautiful frame with no caption is easy to like and hard to hire from. Photographers who get useful conversations on X usually write the constraint: the light, the brief, the lens, the time they had, or the thing the client asked them not to do.

This is for portrait, wedding, documentary, commercial and editorial photographers. Get permission before you post a recognisable person, a private home, or a job a client has not cleared. Do not invent awards, magazine credits or “shot for a famous brand” if it is not true. Do not watermark someone else’s picture.

The caption is part of the work in a text-first feed. Say what the picture will not show: the weather, the five-minute window, the fact that the product had to stay unopened.

Captions and posts that do some work

  • The constraint. Ten minutes on a station platform, mixed strip lights, a child who did not want to sit. That is more useful than “loved this shoot”.
  • A technical note in plain language. Why you chose available light, why you moved, why this lens. Not a gear flex for its own sake.
  • A process post: how you brief, how many images a couple actually receives, when the gallery lands, how you handle raw files.
  • A usage post. What a personal print licence is vs a campaign licence, in one paragraph.
  • An edit decision. Why you cropped, why you left a colour cast, why the “hero” is not the smiliest frame.
  • Availability and rates as facts, when you are ready to say them, not as a teaser.

Example posts you can adapt

Concrete-fact hook · example
This portrait was made in eight minutes, under the platform lights at a commuter station, on a 50mm, no flash.

The brief was: “I have a train at 18:12 and I do not want a studio.” We did four poses and left. Full series up on the site, with the client’s okay.
Question + list · example
If you are hiring a photographer for a campaign, put these in the brief before you ask for a day rate:

1. Where the images will run (web, print, paid ads, packaging).
2. How long you need the licence.
3. Whether you need the raw files (my default is no).
4. The drop-dead delivery date.

Usage is most of the price. The day rate is the rest.
Short twist · example
The sharpest frame from this wedding is not the one I would print.

The one I would print is slightly soft, because she had just seen her father. I will always show both, and I will say which one I stand behind.
Process · example
A standard wedding set with me is: a pre-wedding call, coverage from getting ready to the first part of the evening, and a gallery of 400–600 edited photographs within four weeks.

An album, a second shooter and extra hours are quoted separately. I put all of this in the contract, not in a story.
Edit decision · example
I left the green of the fluorescent lights in this shop portrait on purpose.

Correcting it to “neutral” made the place look like a studio. The job was to show where she actually works.
Usage · example
A personal licence, the way I write it, covers your website, your social accounts and prints for your home or office.

It does not cover paid ads, stock libraries, or selling the image on merch. If you need that, we write a campaign licence with a term and a territory. Ask before you run ads.
Very short hook · example
If the only direction on the brief is “make it pop”, I will send it back.

Tell me where the picture has to live, and what must be readable at the size it will be used.
Permission note · example
I do not post photographs of children from a family session unless a parent has said so in writing, for this use.

This frame is used here with that permission. If you asked me not to post, I will not post, even when the picture is the one I like most.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

What to skip

Do not drag another photographer’s work for engagement. Do not post a sneak from a wedding the couple asked you to keep quiet. Do not use a “before” of a person that would embarrass them. FAQ

Should I post every job?

Only the ones you have clearance for, and only if you have something to say about the constraint or the use. A grid of uncaptioned work is a portfolio; a text feed needs a sentence.

Can I name the client?

When they have agreed. Some brands will want a legal pass. Some families will want no public post at all. Default to asking.

Does gear content work?

A tool note attached to a real job can be useful. A naked unboxing rarely helps you get hired.
