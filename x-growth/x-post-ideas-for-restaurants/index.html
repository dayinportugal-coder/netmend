X post ideas for restaurants

“Open for lunch, book now” is a sign on the door, not a post. Restaurant accounts that people actually follow usually post something you can only know if you work there: today’s special, a supplier, a change to hours, or a honest note about waiting times.

This is for restaurants, cafes, bakeries and bars. It is not a paid-ads plan and it will not fill every table. If you show a plate, use a photo you took today, not a stock image from another kitchen. If you mention a price, it should still be true at service. Do not invent reviews or queue photos.

The useful cadence is small and frequent: one fact about food or hours, posted when you can also reply to a booking question. A monthly brand film does less, for this job, than a Tuesday note that the stew has sold out.

What to post from a kitchen or a pass

  • Today’s special, with the constraint. “We have 18 portions of the mackerel, lunch only, £16.” A real limit is more interesting than an adjective.
  • A supplier or a produce note you can name: the farm, the fish, the bakery that sends the bread on Thursdays.
  • A change to hours, menus or allergy information — dated, and repeated in the usual booking channel too.
  • A booking rule people get wrong: Saturday deposit, walk-in bar seats, how long you hold a table.
  • A behind-the-pass detail that is not staff humiliation: why a dish is off, why a cake only appears on Fridays.
  • A local, specific invitation (match days, a neighbouring gallery’s opening night) rather than “happy hour vibes”.

Example posts you can adapt

Concrete-fact hook · example
Lunch special today: grilled mackerel, new potatoes, the fennel salad. £16. We have 18 portions and we will not 86 it quietly — when it is gone, this post will say so.

Kitchen opens at 12:00. No reservation needed for the counter.
Question + list · example
If you are booking Saturday dinner here, three things that help us:

1. A deposit of £10 a head, returned on the bill.
2. Tell us allergies in the booking, not at the table.
3. We hold a table for 15 minutes, then we release it to the people who are waiting.

The book is at the usual link in the bio.
Short twist · example
We took the cheesecake off the menu this week.

The cream cheese we trust is not available until next Tuesday, and I will not swap in a brand I have not tasted. The tart is still on. Sorry if you came for the cheesecake; it will be back when the cheese is.
Supplier note · example
The tomatoes on the lunch salad this week are from a greenhouse in Formby, picked yesterday.

They are not pretty enough for a stock photo. They taste of something, which is why they are on the plate.
Hours change · example
We are closing at 15:00 this Thursday, 19 November, for a staff meeting.

Breakfast and lunch as usual. Dinner service starts again on Friday at 18:00. If you already have a Thursday evening booking, we have written to you.
Sold-out post · example
The mackerel is gone for today.

The soup and the pork schnitzel are still on. Kitchen until 14:30.
Very short hook · example
Walk-ins: we keep six seats at the bar for dinner, no reservation.

You may wait. We will tell you the honest time, not a hopeful one.
Dish explanation · example
The stew on Wednesday is the same recipe we have used since we opened: beef shin, beer, onions, five hours.

It is not a “new drop”. It is on Wednesdays because that is the day we have time to cook it properly. £14, with bread.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

What to skip

Do not buy fake reviews or post a plate from a previous season as if it were today’s. Do not argue with a public complaint about food safety in a sarcastic tone; take it offline and deal with it. Do not show staff in a way they have not agreed to. Use the free writer for drafts of specials and booking rules, then have the person who runs the floor check the times and prices.

FAQ

Should we post every special?

If the fact is true and you can update the post or reply when it sells out, yes. A stale special is worse than silence.

Can we ask people to tag us?

You can invite it. Do not badger a guest at the table for a photo, and do not repost a guest’s photo without their okay.

Will this replace booking ads?

It can help regulars and neighbours. It is not a paid media plan, and empty Tuesday nights usually need more than a witty post.
