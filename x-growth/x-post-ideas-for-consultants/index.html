X post ideas for consultants

“Thought leadership” that could have been written by anyone in your industry is easy to produce and easy to skip. Consultants who get serious replies on X usually post a diagnostic question, a trade-off they made on a job, or a clear description of what is in and out of scope.

This is for independent and small-firm consultants (operations, strategy, HR, IT, marketing, and similar). Do not invent case-study percentages. Do not name a client unless you have permission. Do not give the impression that a 200-character post is a diagnosis of a company you have not seen.

Write as if a sharp peer is reading: the constraint, the option you rejected, and how you would tell whether the work is done.

Post types that sound like real work

  • A diagnostic question you ask in week one, and why a vague answer is a problem.
  • A trade-off. Speed vs completeness, a pilot vs a full rollout, a tool vs a process change.
  • A definition of done for a common engagement, in observable terms.
  • A scope boundary you now write into proposals, and the overrun that taught you.
  • A method note (how you interview, how you sample, how you report) that a buyer can recognise.
  • What you will not take on, professionally, without drama.

Example posts you can adapt

Concrete-fact hook · example
In the first week of an operations review I ask for the last 20 incidents, not the last slide deck about incidents.

Decks describe the system people wish they had. The tickets describe the one they have. If there is no list, that is already a finding.
Question + list · example
Before you hire a consultant for a “transformation”, I would want written answers to these:

1. What will be true in six months that is not true now?
2. Who has the authority to say no to a workstream?
3. What will you stop doing to make room for this?
4. How will you tell, in numbers you already track, that it worked?

If those cannot be answered, the project is still a slogan.
Short twist · example
A 60-page report is sometimes the least useful thing I can leave behind.

A two-page decision memo, the data behind it, and a meeting with the person who can actually decide: that is often the work. I will still write the long appendix if you need it for a board, but it is not the product.
Trade-off · example
We recommended a four-week pilot in one warehouse rather than a 12-site rollout.

The client wanted the big bang, because the board date was fixed. The pilot found a labelling problem that would have been copied 12 times. I will keep making this argument, even when it is unfashionable.
Scope · example
My proposals now say, in writing: I do not run the client’s internal comms, and I do not sit as an unofficial manager of their staff.

I will design the work, run the workshops we agreed, and hand over the artefacts. If you need an interim manager, that is a different contract.
Definition of done · example
For a pricing project, “done” for me is: a documented price list, the rules for exceptions, an owner of those rules, and a first month of invoices that follow them.

A workshop and a slide are not done. They are a start.
Very short hook · example
If nobody in the room can name the decision the workshop is meant to produce, I will cancel the workshop.

We can meet when the decision is written down, even if the answer is still blank.
What I will not take · example
I do not take “come and motivate the team” as a brief.

I will look at the system they work in: targets, tools, hand-offs, staffing. If the brief is only a pep talk, I am the wrong hire.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

FAQ

Should I post frameworks I use with clients?

Yes if they are yours to share and they do not expose a client’s data. A named sequence with steps is more useful than a 2×2 you never use.

Is a newsletter better than X?

They do different jobs. X is a public notebook and a place to be found. A newsletter is for people who already want more. You can point from one to the other without turning every post into a signup ad.

Will this replace business development?

It can support it. It is not a substitute for conversations with people who already know your work.
