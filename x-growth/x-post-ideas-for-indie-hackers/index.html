X post ideas for indie hackers

Build-in-public went stale when every post became a template: day count, a revenue graph, a lesson that could apply to anyone. The posts that still help are dated ships, decisions, and numbers you would also stand behind if a customer asked.

This is for solo and tiny-team builders. You do not have to post revenue. If you do, it should be real, and you should be ready for it to be screenshotted for years. Do not invent a waitlist count. Do not imply a dying product is “exploding”.

Write the unit of work: what shipped, what broke, what you will do next week, and one thing you are not building.

What to post when you are the whole company

  • A ship note with a date and a limitation.
  • A decision (pricing, a killed feature, a channel you stopped) and the reason.
  • A number with a denominator: 12 paying customers out of 80 trials, not “growth is up”.
  • A support or onboarding insight from real users, without exposing them.
  • A stack or cost note if it is useful (what you pay for hosting this month) — optional, not a flex.
  • A ask that is specific: “I need two people who invoice in euros to try this export”, not “feedback welcome”.

Example posts you can adapt

Concrete-fact hook · example
Shipped this afternoon: CSV export of invoices, UTF-8, with a decimal comma if the workspace is set to Europe.

It does not yet include credit notes. That is next week. If you invoice in euros and you have 20 minutes, I would like you to break it. The link is in my bio.
Question + list · example
I killed the public roadmap this week, for three reasons:

1. People treated it as a promise.
2. Competitors copied the titles, which I do not actually mind, but customers were confused when dates slipped.
3. I was writing the roadmap instead of the work.

Requests still come in by email. I will ship, then say what shipped.
Short twist · example
I have 19 paying customers, not 190.

I would rather write that sentence than post a graph I have to crop. 19 is enough to talk to, and not enough to pretend I have product-market fit as a slogan.
Onboarding · example
Three of the last five trial users stalled on the same screen: they could not tell whether to add a client first or an invoice first.

I put “add a client” in the empty state and hid the other buttons until one client exists. I will report in a fortnight whether the drop-off moved, not tonight.
Costs · example
This product’s bill this month: $17 hosting, $0 for email (still inside the free tier), and my time.

When email goes over the free tier I will post the new number. I will not invent a “stack” for a screenshot.
Specific ask · example
I need two bookkeepers who already use bank CSV files to try a mapping screen.

It will take 15 minutes, on a dummy file if you prefer. I will watch, I will not sell you anything on the call, and I will pay a $40 thank-you. Reply or email the address in my bio.
Very short hook · example
If I cannot explain the product in one sentence that names the user and the file they already have, I am not ready to run ads.

I am still on that sentence. I am not running ads.
Decision · example
I am not adding a mobile app this year.

The work is a desktop browser job (tables, CSV, invoices). An app would be a second product I cannot support. If that means I lose people who only want to work on a phone, I will lose them.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

FAQ

Do I have to post revenue?

No. Plenty of serious builders never do. If you do, be consistent and honest, including on the quiet months.

Is a daily build log required?

No. A weekly ship note is enough for most people. Daily posts that say “still coding” do not help anyone.

Can I post user quotes?

With permission, and without editing them into a testimonial they did not write.
