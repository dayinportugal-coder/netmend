X post ideas for personal trainers

Transformation photos and “shred in six weeks” lines are the most common posts in this industry, and the least trustworthy. Trainers who earn attention on X usually post a regression, a session structure, a habit that can be done without a gadget, or a clear limit of what they are qualified to say.

This is for personal trainers, strength coaches and similar fitness professionals. It is not medical advice. Do not diagnose, do not prescribe diets you are not qualified to prescribe, and do not post a client’s body without explicit permission. Do not invent before-and-after results or promise a weight or a time.

Write the session, the regression, and the person it is for (a beginner, a runner coming back from a break, someone with a sore knee who has already seen a clinician). Leave the rest to the programme you actually sell offline.

Useful post types

  • A session in outline: the main lift or movement, the sets you actually use, the rest, and a regression.
  • A form cue in one sentence, attached to a movement, not a dunk on a stranger’s video.
  • A habit post that does not require a supplement: steps, protein at breakfast if that is in your scope, sleep, walking on rest days.
  • A return-to-training note after illness or a break (general, conservative, “get cleared if you need to”).
  • How you work: in-person or online, how long a block is, what you need from a new client (PAR-Q, a recent session history).
  • A limit. What you refer out: pain, eating disorders, medical conditions.

Example posts you can adapt

Concrete-fact hook · example
Today’s beginner strength session, 45 minutes:

1. Goblet squat, 3×8, 90 seconds rest.
2. Chest-supported row, 3×8.
3. Split squat holding the rack, 3×6 each side.
4. Carry, 3 trips of 20 metres.

If the goblet squat is unstable, we swap a sit-to-stand to a box. That is the session, not a secret programme.
Question + list · example
If your knees complain on lunges, I usually check these three things (after you have seen a clinician if the pain is sharp or new):

1. Can you do a split squat holding a rack, shorter range?
2. Do you have enough room to stay upright?
3. Are you adding load before you can control the descent?

I will not diagnose you on X. If it hurts in a way that worries you, get it looked at.
Short twist · example
You do not need a new programme every Monday.

You need the same handful of movements, loaded a little more carefully, for long enough to see whether they are working. A new PDF is not progress.
Regression · example
If a press-up at the floor is a collapse, we do not “just do more of them”.

We raise the hands to a bench or a rail, keep a straight line from head to heels, and earn the floor later. The raised press-up is still a press-up.
Habit · example
A rest-day walk is part of the plan I write, not an optional extra.

Twenty to thirty minutes, easy pace, outside if you can. It is not a punishment for missing the gym, and it does not need a tracking badge.
Scope limit · example
I am a personal trainer, not a physiotherapist or a doctor.

If you have unexplained pain, dizziness, chest pain, or a condition you have not had cleared for exercise, I will ask you to speak to a clinician before we train. That is the job, not a refusal to help.
Very short hook · example
If the warm-up is the only time your hips feel organised, the warm-up is doing work the session should also do.

We keep a couple of those drills and we put them in the work sets, lighter, until they stick.
How I work · example
A typical block with me is 12 weeks, once or twice a week in the room, plus a short plan for the days I do not see you.

I ask for a recent training history and a health screen first. I do not take on “get a holiday body in 21 days”. We can talk about a date; we will not invent a timeline.

These are original example drafts, written for this page, for a fictional professional. They are not real posts from a real account, and they are not a promise of reach, clients or sales. Adapt the structure; replace every fact with one you can stand behind.

What to skip

No body-shaming, no “no excuses”, no supplement miracle claims, no client photos without permission, no form-shaming a stranger’s video. Use the free writer for session copy if you want, then check that every regression is one you would actually coach.

FAQ

Can I post client results?

Only with permission, only real numbers, and without implying everyone will get the same outcome. Prefer talking about the programme rather than someone’s body.

Is a daily workout post enough?

It can help. It is stronger if you add who it is for and a regression. A raw list of exercises is easy to scroll past and easy to do badly.

Should I argue about nutrition online?

Stay inside your qualification. Refer out when the question is medical or clinical. Public fights about diets rarely help your clients.
