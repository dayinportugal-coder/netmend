CSV vs Excel for bank statements

What each format is

A CSV (comma-separated values, or semicolon-separated in much of Europe) is a text file. One row per line, one column per field, nothing else — no colours, no formulas, no hidden sheets, no number formats. QuickBooks Online’s official bank upload, Xero’s official CSV bank import, and most “upload a spreadsheet” screens are built around this. Our converter downloads a CSV, not an .xlsx, for that reason.

An Excel workbook (.xlsx) can hold several tabs, formulas, filters and formatting. It is the better file for a human review: a second tab that proves opening + movements = closing, a SUMIF per invoice, a colour on a row that failed the check. It is a worse interchange format, because the next program may not read the formulas, and because Excel itself may have already rewritten “03-09-2026” or “01/02” the last time someone double-clicked the file. Some banks’ “Excel” downloads are actually CSVs with an .xls name (Revolut is a documented example). Always look at the real file type.

When CSV is the right thing to hand over

  • You are importing into QuickBooks, Xero, Google Sheets, or any tool with a column mapper.
  • You want a file that will still open in ten years without a particular version of Excel.
  • You need European and US/UK systems to agree on separators: you can choose comma or semicolon deliberately, and say so to the person who will open it.

When Excel is the right thing to keep

  • You are reviewing a packed year, and you want a proof tab, frozen headers, and a filter on the description.
  • You need two views of the same data (raw extract, and a cleaned import tab).
  • You will do further work — VAT analysis, a running balance, a pivot — before anyone imports anything.

The practical habit is: convert or download to CSV, immediately save a working copy as .xlsx, and never do the cleaning in the only copy of the CSV. When the proof tab is clean, File → Export or Save As CSV from the import tab, and upload that. If you open a CSV in Excel to “just tidy the dates”, you are in the danger zone that official import guides keep warning about: a US Excel will turn a UK 05/03/2026 into 3 May, and it will do it silently.

Google Sheets sits in the middle. It imports CSV well if you set the separator and the locale yourself — see PDF invoices to Google Sheets — but it has the same date-locale trap. The converter below gives you the CSV; what you do with it next is the choice this page is about.

FAQ

Which format does QuickBooks want?

A CSV in the official 3-column or 4-column layout, or a QBO/QFX/OFX file from the bank. Not a formatted Excel workbook with a proof tab still in it.

Which format does Xero want?

A CSV you can map on the official import screen (or, when it is offered, Xero’s own PDF statement import). An .xlsx full of formulas is not the starting point.

Why does my CSV look like one column in Excel?

It is probably semicolon-separated and Excel expected commas, or the other way around. Use Data → From Text/CSV and pick the delimiter, instead of double-clicking the file.
