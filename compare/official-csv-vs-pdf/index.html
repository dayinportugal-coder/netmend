What the two files actually are

An official transaction export (CSV, XLS, QBO, QFX, MT940, CAMT.053, midata) is a data file the bank generated from the same ledger as the app. It is usually the cleanest set of columns, and it is what accounting tools are built to import. It is not always a statement. Some banks say so in as many words: Starling calls its feed-export CSV a transaction summary, not a verifiable monthly statement; Nationwide midata is a comparison-site file that deliberately omits the last 31 days; Commerzbank distinguishes an Umsatz CSV from the Kontoauszug in the Postfach; U.S. Bank lets you download three years of transactions but seven years of e-statement PDFs.

An official statement PDF is the document with the letterhead, the period, and the printed opening and closing balances. It is what a lender, a landlord, an auditor or a tax enquiry usually asks for. It is a poor working file until someone turns the table into rows. That is the only job a converter has.

When the official export is the right choice

  • The dates you need are inside the export window (18 months of ABN AMRO XLS, two years of American Express Excel/CSV, three years of U.S. Bank CSV/QBO, twelve months of a Starling feed export, and so on).
  • You are importing into software that already understands that file (QBO into QuickBooks, official CSV into Xero, MT940 or CAMT.053 into an accountant’s tool).
  • You do not need the printed “True Copy” or letterhead in the same file — you can keep the PDF in a folder and work from the export.

When converting the PDF is the right choice

  • The bank never offered a spreadsheet, or the official export does not go back far enough.
  • You were only sent the PDF (a client, a tenant, a solicitor).
  • You need the rows to tie out to a specific statement: opening balance + movements = closing balance, with no missing 31-day gap and no mid-month feed that includes pending items.
  • The official export is a summary without a running balance, and you want a check column you can tick against the PDF.

A converted PDF is only as good as the layout and the check. A text-based official PDF usually converts cleanly. A scan does not. And a conversion is not a certified copy of the statement — if a third party asked for the statement, send the official PDF, and keep the spreadsheet for the books.

FAQ

Is the official CSV “more accurate” than the PDF?

They should come from the same ledger, but they are not the same document. The PDF is the statement, with printed balances. The CSV is a data extract, sometimes for a different date range, and sometimes without pending or end-of-weekend items. Tick whichever file you use against the question you are trying to answer.

Can I throw the PDF away if I have the CSV?

Not if someone may ask for an official statement. Keep the PDF. Use the CSV (or a conversion) as the working file.

Which should I import into Xero or QuickBooks?

The bank’s official QBO, QFX or CSV, or a live feed, when it covers the dates. Convert the PDF only when that file does not exist or does not go back far enough, then reshape it to the official import layout.
