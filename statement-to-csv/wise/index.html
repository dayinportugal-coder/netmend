The Wise statement format

DatesThe official CSV writes dates day-first as DD-MM-YYYY with no locale marker (03-09-2026 is 3 September 2026). A generic spreadsheet in a US locale will swap any day below 13 into the wrong month. PDF statements follow the language you pick when you generate the file.
NumbersOfficial CSV: comma-separated, dot decimals (1234.56). European Excel may still split columns wrongly if you double-click the file — use Data → From Text/CSV.

Where to download the official file

Official path, from the Wise Help Centre (“How do I download a statement?”):
  1. Open the currency or jar, or click your initials and choose Statements and reports → Statements.
  2. Select Create a statement. You can choose currencies or jars, a period, a file format and a language.
  3. Enter your Wise password to confirm. For accounting statements, tick the box to show fees separately.

Formats on the web: PDF, XLSX, CSV, MT940, QIF or CAMT.053 (version 10). In the app: PDF, CSV and XLSX. Each statement covers at most 365 days — longer periods need several files. Statements only include activity on Wise currency accounts (not payments made with an external card). You can stamp and sign a PDF after address verification.

What the PDF contains

A Wise PDF is the official branded statement for one balance (or several, if you selected more than one currency). It lists the period, opening and closing balances, and the transactions for that balance. Descriptions often wrap onto a second line; card merchants can be buried inside a sentence.

Official CSV or Excel, when the bank offers one

A typical personal CSV is comma-separated with around 23 columns. The first line looks like: "TransferWise ID",Date,"Date Time",Amount,Currency,Description,"Payment Reference","Running Balance","Exchange From","Exchange To","Exchange Rate","Payer Name","Payee Name","Payee Account Number",Merchant,"Card Last Four Digits","Card Holder Full Name",Attachment,Note,"Total fees","Exchange To Amount","Transaction Type","Transaction Details Type"

  • Date is the completion date, day-first (DD-MM-YYYY).
  • Amount is signed. Total fees is a separate column.
  • Merchant usually holds a cleaner shop name than Description.
  • Business exports may add columns such as Batch ID or Reference.

Do not open the CSV “to tidy the dates” in Excel before you import it — that is the edit that most often silently rewrites 03-09 into 9 March.

What to watch for

  • Prefer the official CSV or XLSX over a PDF conversion whenever you can still log in.
  • Download each currency separately, or generate a multi-currency zip from Statements and keep the files apart.
  • If you only have the PDF, set this tool to UK/US numbers and a comma CSV, then check a date after the 12th of the month so you can see whether the day and month landed the right way round.

FAQ

Does Wise already export CSV?

Yes — and also XLSX, MT940, QIF and CAMT.053 on the web. Convert a PDF only when that official export is no longer available.

Why are my months wrong after I open the file?

Wise writes 03-09-2026 for 3 September. Excel in a US locale reads that as 9 March. Import the column as text or day-first, or look at a date on the 13th or later to see the real order.

Can I get more than a year in one file?

No. Official statements are capped at 365 days. Create several statements and concatenate them after you have checked each period.
