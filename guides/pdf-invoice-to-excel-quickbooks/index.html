What QuickBooks actually imports

There are two different jobs, and they use two different files. Do not mix them up.

  • Invoices you sent (sales). In QuickBooks Online, go to Settings → Import data → Invoices. Official help (updated recently, checked 13/11/2026) says each line item needs its own invoice number, customer, invoice date, due date, item amount and item tax code. You can use your own CSV if those columns are there, or download Intuit’s sample file from that screen. One spreadsheet can contain up to 1,000 rows and you can import a maximum of 100 invoices at a time. You cannot add negative charges such as discounts or credit notes on this import. Add new customers or products first, or tick the boxes on the import screen to create them. If you collect tax, map your tax codes to QuickBooks tax codes and choose Exclusive or Inclusive. If some invoices fail, Intuit tells you to note why, and if the file itself looks corrupted, to copy the data into a new CSV and try again.
  • Bills you received (purchases). That is a separate import under Settings → Import data → Bills. Download Intuit’s sample file from that screen and treat those headings as the authority — do not reuse an invoices template for bills, or a bank-transaction template for either of them.
  • Bank or card transactions. A third, different file. QuickBooks Online accepts a 3-column CSV (Date, Description, Amount, with payments negative and deposits positive) or a 4-column CSV (Date, Description, Credit, Debit). That is for money that already moved, not for raising an invoice. Official formatting notes: remove stray zeros, do not put amounts in the Description column, Credit/Debit headers must not say “amount”, keep one date format (Intuit recommends dd/mm/yyyy), and strip weekday text such as “TUE” from the date. Mac users should save as a Windows CSV. For card accounts, upload into a credit-card account, not a bank account — payments often appear as negative because they reduce the card balance.

Connecting the bank feed, when QuickBooks offers it, is usually better than any of these files for ongoing bookkeeping. Use a spreadsheet import when the feed does not go back far enough, or when you have a pile of PDFs and no feed.

How to get line items out of a PDF invoice

The free tool on this page is the same in-browser converter we built for bank statements. It reads the text layer of a digital PDF on your computer (no upload). It works when the invoice is a real text PDF with a line-item table — description, quantity, unit price, line total. It does not work well on a photographed invoice, a logo-heavy layout with the totals in a random place, or a scanned page. For those, type the lines or use the done-for-you option below.

  1. Save the supplier’s PDF (or our sample invoices, if you just want to see the tool).
  2. Drop it in the box below. If the preview looks messy, switch to Every text row (raw table) so wrapped descriptions are not dropped.
  3. Download the CSV and reshape it in Excel or Google Sheets to the QuickBooks invoice or bills template: one row per line item, the invoice number repeated on every line of the same invoice, dates in one format, tax codes that exist in your company file, no discount rows as negatives.
  4. Tick each invoice total against the PDF before you import. Then import from Settings → Import data, and map the columns on the screen.

If you only need a payment to match a bank line, you do not import the invoice as a bank transaction. Raise or import the invoice or bill first, then match the bank feed to it. Importing the same amount twice — once as an invoice and once as a bank split — is how balances go wrong.

FAQ

Can QuickBooks import a PDF invoice directly?

Not as an invoice or bill. QuickBooks Online imports a CSV (or its sample spreadsheet) of invoices or bills, and a separate CSV or bank-feed file for transactions. You convert the PDF first, then map the columns.

How many invoices can I import at once?

Official help: up to 100 invoices per import, and up to 1,000 rows in the spreadsheet. Discounts and credit notes cannot go through this invoice import as negative charges.

Should I import invoices or bank transactions?

If the invoice is a sale or a bill you need on the books, import it as an invoice or a bill. If the money has already hit the bank and you only need the feed to match, use the bank CSV or the live bank feed — not the invoice template.
