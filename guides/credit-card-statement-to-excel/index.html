Use the issuer’s spreadsheet when they offer one

Some card issuers already export activity as Excel or CSV. American Express (US), for example, officially lets you download the previous two years of transactions as PDF, Excel or CSV, and to fetch an official billing-statement PDF (archived statements can take up to 24 hours). When that official download covers your dates, use it. Convert the billing-statement PDF when the period is older, when you were only sent the PDF, or when you need the rows to tick against that statement’s front-page totals.

Bank-issued cards (Capital One, Chase, Halifax and others) usually keep the official document as a monthly PDF under statements. A live activity export, if it exists, is not always the same as the official statement. Our bank guides say, bank by bank, what is official.

What to watch for on a card PDF

  • The summary is not a transaction. Previous balance, payments received, new charges, fees, interest, the amount due and the payment date belong on the front page. If you leave “new charges 1,234.00” in the CSV as a row, you will double the spend.
  • Payments and refunds have the opposite effect from a purchase. In QuickBooks Online, Intuit tells you to upload card activity into a credit-card account, not a bank account, because the signs often look “backwards” (a payment can appear negative because it reduces the balance). In Xero, map the amounts so a purchase increases the card balance and a payment decreases it, then review the import before you accept it.
  • Instalment plans, interest and fees are real rows, but they are not purchases. Keep them, and check that purchases + fees + interest − payments − credits = the new balance on the statement.
  • Pending authorisations can appear in an app feed and not on the official statement. Convert the official PDF, not a screenshot of recent activity, if you need the file to tie out.

Convert, then prove the statement

The tool below reads a digital (text-based) statement PDF in your browser. It will not read a photographed bill. Choose UK/US or European numbers to match the PDF. Then:

  1. Delete the summary block and any “amount due” lines from the CSV. Keep payments, purchases, refunds, fees and interest.
  2. Prove the identity: previous balance + purchases + fees + interest − payments − credits = new balance, using the figures on the PDF. If this fails, a summary total is still in the file, or a wrapped merchant name has been read as an amount.
  3. For QuickBooks, reshape to the official 3-column or 4-column bank CSV and upload into the card account. For Xero, use the official CSV bank import (or Xero’s own PDF import if it accepts this issuer). For a spreadsheet you will only review, the raw columns are fine.

FAQ

Is a card statement the same as a bank statement for import?

No. Upload it as a credit-card account. A payment reduces the card balance; treating it as a current-account withdrawal will invert your books.

Does American Express already give me Excel?

On US accounts, official help says yes: the previous two years of transactions can be downloaded as PDF, Excel or CSV. The official monthly statement is still the billing-statement PDF.

Why don’t the rows add up to the new balance?

Usually a front-page total was kept as a transaction, or interest and fees were dropped. Rebuild the identity from the statement summary before you import.
