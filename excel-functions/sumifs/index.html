What SUMIFS does

The standard way to total by category and date, or by account and row number (for a running balance). The argument order is not the same as SUMIF.

Adds numbers that meet several conditions at once.

Syntax and a worked example in 6 languages

English Excel uses commas between arguments and a dot decimal. Most European Excel versions use semicolons and a decimal comma. TRUE / FALSE also change name. The translator below applies all three.

English — SUMIFS
=SUMIFS(sum_range, criteria_range1, criteria1, [criteria_range2, criteria2], …)
=SUMIFS(C:C,B:B,"Rent",A:A,">="&DATE(2026,1,1))
Português (PT) — SOMA.SE.S
=SOMA.SE.S(sum_range; criteria_range1; criteria1; [criteria_range2; criteria2]; …)
=SOMA.SE.S(C:C;B:B;"Rent";A:A;">="&DATA(2026;1;1))
Español — SUMAR.SI.CONJUNTO
=SUMAR.SI.CONJUNTO(sum_range; criteria_range1; criteria1; [criteria_range2; criteria2]; …)
=SUMAR.SI.CONJUNTO(C:C;B:B;"Rent";A:A;">="&FECHA(2026;1;1))

Common mistakes

  • SUMIFS puts the sum range first. SUMIF puts it last (and makes it optional). Mixing the two is the usual #VALUE! or a plausible but wrong total.
  • Every criteria range must be the same size as the sum range.
  • A running balance per account in older Excel is often two SUMIFS (money in minus money out) with a “row number <= this row” condition. It works, but it gets heavy on very long lists; a helper column per account can be faster.

FAQ

What is SUMIFS in Portuguese?

SOMA.SE.S. Spanish SUMAR.SI.CONJUNTO, French SOMME.SI.ENS, German SUMMEWENNS, Italian SOMMA.PIÙ.SE.

Why is the argument order different from SUMIF?

Historical accident. Remember: SUMIFS starts with the numbers you are adding.

How do I do a running balance by account?

Add a row-number helper column, then SUMIFS of Money in minus SUMIFS of Money out, both filtered to that account and to rows up to the current one. Transfers between accounts should be two rows (out of A, into B), not one.
