What SUMIF does

The everyday “total of this category” formula, under SOMA.SE, SUMAR.SI, SOMME.SI, SUMMEWENN, SOMMA.SE.

Adds the numbers in a range that meet one condition.

Syntax and a worked example in 6 languages

English Excel uses commas between arguments and a dot decimal. Most European Excel versions use semicolons and a decimal comma. TRUE / FALSE also change name. The translator below applies all three.

English — SUMIF
=SUMIF(range, criteria, [sum_range])
=SUMIF(B:B,"Rent",C:C)
Português (PT) — SOMA.SE

Common mistakes

  • If you fill the formula down a table, lock the ranges ($B:$B and $C:$C). An unlocked SUMIF that shifts one row at a time is a classic silent error.
  • For several conditions (category and date, or category and project), use SUMIFS, not a second SUMIF.
  • Criteria that refer to a cell need concatenation: ">="&F1, not ">=F1".

FAQ

What is SUMIF in Portuguese?

SOMA.SE. Spanish SUMAR.SI, French SOMME.SI, German SUMMEWENN, Italian SOMMA.SE.

Can I use two conditions?

Use SUMIFS (SOMA.SE.S, SUMAR.SI.CONJUNTO, SOMME.SI.ENS, SUMMEWENNS, SOMMA.PIÙ.SE).

Text or numbers in the criteria?

Text goes in quotes. A number can be bare or in quotes. Cell references should be concatenated with the operator.
