A blog post about “growth” does not get you reach.

If the first line is “I just wanted to share” and the rest is five hashtags plus a link, most people never get to the point. Put the point first; leave the link for a reply.

FAQ

Is this the official X algorithm?

No. Nobody outside X knows the exact ranking weights, and they change. This tool is a checklist of common problems in the text (a weak hook, a link in the main post, a hashtag stack, engagement bait, and so on), plus an optional rewrite. It cannot see your analytics, your audience, or the media on the post, and it does not predict impressions.

Do I need a Gemini API key?

Not for the local checks. Those run in your browser with no key and no daily cap. You only need a key if you want the AI diagnosis and the 3 rewrites. Google offers a free Gemini API key through Google AI Studio. Limits and availability are Google’s, not ours, and they can change. We do not resell keys and we do not have a paid Google account sitting behind this page.

Where is my key stored? Do you see my posts?

The key is saved in localStorage in your browser. Your post is sent only to Google’s Gemini API, from your browser, with that key, and only when you click the rewrite button. Netmend does not receive the key, the post, or the rewrite. There is no Netmend server for this tool. On a shared computer, use Remove key when you finish. If you already saved a key in X Post Writer in this same browser, it is reused.

What counts toward the daily limit?

One click of “Diagnose and rewrite with Gemini”. The free version allows 3 of those per day in this browser. Local checks do not count. The count is kept in localStorage, so it is per browser, not per person. Clearing the site’s data resets it, which we do not try to stop, but the fair use is 3 AI rewrites a day.

Will this post to X for me?

No. It checks the text and, if you ask, writes a rewrite. You copy the version you are happy with and post it yourself.

Yes. The local checks for length, links, hashtags, mentions and formatting work in any language. Some hook and CTA phrases are English-specific, so those items may be less precise. If you ask for a rewrite, the model is told to write in the same language as the original post.

What the local checks look at

These are the items the browser runs without a key. They are structures and patterns, not a ranking formula.

HookThe first line has to stand alone. Filler, a bare link, a hashtag or an @ at the start is flagged.
LengthVery short posts leave little to reply to. Posts over 280 characters need a reason to be that long.
External linksA link in the main post often travels less well than the same text with the link in a reply.
HashtagsZero or one is usually fine. A stack of tags is treated as a problem.
FormattingWalls of text, all-caps, emoji piles, repeated punctuation, and em dashes.
CTA“Like if you agree” and similar bait are flagged. A specific question is not.
MentionsA long tag list, or a post that starts with @ and may be treated as a reply.
TimingOptional. A quiet window in the timezone you name is flagged. This is not a best-time calculator.
